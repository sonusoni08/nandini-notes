Double Fertilization

🌸 What is Double Fertilization?

A unique process in angiosperms where two sperms are involved in two separate fusion events inside the embryo sac. First discovered by S.G. Nawaschin (1898).

  • Pollen tube delivers 2 sperms into the embryo sac
  • Tube bursts inside a synergid
  • Two distinct fusion events occur simultaneously

🌺 Syngamy (Generative Fertilization)

  • Sperm 1 (n) + Egg cell (n) → Zygote (2n)
  • Zygote → develops into the embryo
  • Also called true fertilization
  • Gives rise to the next generation sporophyte

🍑 Triple Fusion

  • Sperm 2 (n) + 2 Polar nuclei (n+n) → Primary Endosperm Nucleus (3n)
  • PEN → divides to form endosperm
  • Endosperm nourishes the developing embryo
  • This 3n nature is unique to flowering plants

🌿 Significance

  • Ensures every fertilized egg is provisioned with food
  • Endosperm forms only when fertilization occurs → no resource waste
  • Triploid endosperm = nutritional innovation of angiosperms
  • Basis of economic crops — rice, wheat, maize endosperm
💡 Remember: Double fertilization = Syngamy (egg + sperm₁ → 2n zygote) + Triple fusion (2 polar nuclei + sperm₂ → 3n PEN). It is exclusive to angiosperms!

Seed Structure — Monocot & Dicot

🌷 "A seed is a tiny miracle — it carries the blueprint of an entire plant!" 🌷

🌾 Monocot Seed (e.g., Maize/Wheat)

  • Seed coat — fused with fruit wall (pericarp) in grains
  • Endosperm — large, floury; main food reserve
  • Aleurone layer — protein-rich outer endosperm layer
  • Embryo — small, shield-shaped
  • Scutellum (single cotyledon) — absorbs nutrients from endosperm
  • Plumule — enclosed in coleoptile
  • Radicle — enclosed in coleorhiza
  • Epiblast — small flap opposite scutellum

🫘 Dicot Seed (e.g., Bean/Gram)

  • Testa (outer seed coat) — protective, hard
  • Tegmen (inner seed coat) — thin, membranous
  • Hilum — scar of attachment to funicle
  • Micropyle — small pore for water entry
  • Two cotyledons — thick, fleshy food storage
  • Embryo axis: epicotyl (above) + hypocotyl (below)
  • Plumule — shoot tip
  • Radicle — root tip

Endospermic & Non-Endospermic Seeds

🍬 Endospermic (Albuminous) Seeds

  • Endosperm persists in the mature seed
  • Cotyledons thin and papery
  • Endosperm = main food reserve used during germination
  • Scutellum secretes amylases to digest endosperm starch

Examples: Zea mays (maize), wheat, rice, barley, castor, coconut

🫐 Non-Endospermic (Exalbuminous) Seeds

  • Endosperm consumed by the embryo during development
  • Cotyledons become thick and swollen with stored food
  • Cotyledons = primary food reserve
  • Cotyledons photosynthesize if they emerge above ground

Examples: Pisum sativum (pea), bean, groundnut, mustard

🥥 Special Case — Coconut

  • Liquid endosperm → coconut water (free nuclear stage)
  • Solid white endosperm → coconut meat/kernel (cellular stage)
  • Both are edible endosperms at different developmental stages
  • The coconut "fruit" is a drupe; the "nut" is the seed

Types of Fruits

🍓 "Fruit is nature's way of saying: well done, you flowered beautifully!" 🍓

🍑 Simple (True) Fruits — from single pistil

🍑

Drupe (Stone fruit)

Fleshy pericarp; hard endocarp (stone)

Mango, plum, peach, coconut

🍎

Berry

Entire pericarp fleshy; seeds embedded

Tomato, banana, grape, brinjal

🍊

Hesperidium

Berry with leathery rind (flavedo+albedo)

Orange, lemon, lime

🍈

Pepo

Berry with hard outer rind; inferior ovary

Cucumber, pumpkin, watermelon

🍐

Pome (False fruit)

Fleshy thalamus; true fruit = core

Apple, pear, quince

🫘

Legume / Pod

Dry dehiscent; monocarpellary; splits both sides

Pea, bean, Acacia

🌾

Caryopsis (Grain)

Pericarp fused with seed coat

Wheat, rice, maize

🌿

Capsule

Dry, dehiscent, pluricarpellary

Cotton, poppy, Datura

🍓 Aggregate & Composite (False) Fruits

🍓 Aggregate Fruit (Etaerio)

Develops from a polycarpellary apocarpous flower; collection of fruitlets.

  • Etaerio of achenes — Strawberry (thalamus fleshy), Rose
  • Etaerio of drupes — Raspberry, Rubus
  • Etaerio of berries — Annona (custard apple)
  • Etaerio of follicles — Magnolia

🍍 Composite (Multiple) Fruit — Sorosis & Syconus

  • Sorosis — develops from catkin inflorescence
    Mulberry, pineapple, jackfruit
  • Syconus — develops from hypanthodium inflorescence; hollow receptacle with flowers inside
    Fig (Ficus)
  • Both involve the entire inflorescence, not just one flower

Seed Dispersal Mechanisms

🌼 "Every journey begins with being carried somewhere new — just like seeds!" 🌼

💨 Anemochory — Wind Dispersal

Adaptations:

  • Wings — Acer (maple), Shorea
  • Hairs/pappus — Calotropis, dandelion, Gossypium
  • Inflated/light fruits — Colutea
  • Dust-like seeds — orchids
  • Ballooning — Physalis

💧 Hydrochory — Water Dispersal

  • Fibrous mesocarp for buoyancy — coconut
  • Spongy pericarp — Nelumbo (lotus)
  • Air chambers — water lilies
  • Cork-like seed coat — Entada
  • Seeds of riparian plants have watertight coats

🐦 Zoochory — Animal Dispersal

  • Epizoochory — hooks/spines stick to fur
    Xanthium, Urena, bur grasses
  • Endozoochory — eaten & excreted
    Berries, drupes (birds, mammals)
  • Synzoochory — hoarded by animals
    Acorns, nuts (squirrels)
  • Myrmecochory — ants carry elaiosome-bearing seeds

💥 Autochory — Self Dispersal

  • Explosive dehiscence — Impatiens, Ecballium (squirting cucumber)
  • Geocarpy — fruit buried underground — groundnut
  • Lomentum — breaks into segments
  • Hura crepitans — seeds shot up to 14 m!
🌸 Fun fact: Coconut can travel thousands of kilometres across oceans and still germinate — one of nature's greatest travellers!

Biology of Endosperm — Structure, Types & Functions

📖 What is Endosperm?

A triploid (3n) nutritive tissue formed after triple fusion. It develops from the Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN) and nourishes the embryo.

  • Found only in angiosperms
  • Can be starchy, oily, or proteinaceous
  • May persist in mature seed (endospermic) or be consumed (non-endospermic)

🏷️ Types of Endosperm Development

TypeFeatureExample
NuclearFree nuclear divisions; cell wall forms laterCoconut water, most dicots
CellularCell wall after every division; no free nucleiAdoxa, Peperomia
HelobialFirst division → 2 unequal cells; then nuclearMonocots (e.g., Helobiales)

⚙️ Functions of Endosperm

  • Main nutritive tissue for embryo development
  • Stores starch, proteins, oils and vitamins
  • Provides enzymes (amylases, proteases) for seed germination
  • Aleurone layer in grasses — secretes GA-induced hydrolases
  • Commercial use: wheat flour (starchy endosperm), coconut milk

Dicot & Monocot Embryo

🌿 "The embryo is the universe in miniature — carrying every instruction for life!" 🌿

🌱 Embryogeny — General Steps

1

Zygote (2n)

Undergoes first asymmetric division → apical cell + basal cell

2

Proembryo

Apical cell → embryo proper; basal cell → suspensor (haustorial)

3

Globular Embryo

Spherical mass of cells; protoderm differentiates

4

Heart-shaped (Dicot)

Two cotyledon primordia appear; embryo axis visible

5

Torpedo / Mature Embryo

Cotyledons elongate; radicle, plumule, hypocotyl fully formed

🫘 Dicot Embryo (e.g., Capsella)

  • Two cotyledons — large, fleshy food reserves
  • Epicotyl — above cotyledon node → forms shoot
  • Plumule — embryonic shoot tip
  • Hypocotyl — between cotyledons & radicle
  • Radicle — embryonic root; enclosed by root cap
  • Suspensor: large basal cell (hypophysis) → forms root cap

🌾 Monocot Embryo (e.g., Grass)

  • One cotyledon = scutellum (shield-shaped)
  • Coleoptile — sheath protecting plumule
  • Coleorhiza — sheath protecting radicle
  • Epiblast — small projection opposite scutellum
  • No hypocotyl; mesocotyl = internode between scutellum and coleoptile
  • Scutellum absorbs nutrients from endosperm

Polyembryony

💡 Definition

The occurrence of more than one embryo in a single seed. First observed by Leeuwenhoek (1719) in citrus.

  • Common in: Citrus, Mangifera, Opuntia
  • Extra embryos = nucellar embryos (most common source)

🌸 Causes of Polyembryony

  • Cleavage polyembryony — zygote splits into multiple embryos
  • Nucellar polyembryony — nucellar cells develop into embryos (adventive)
  • Synergid embryos — synergid develops parthenogenetically
  • Multiple embryo sacs — each may be fertilized

🌿 Significance

  • Nucellar embryos are genetically identical to mother (clonal)
  • Used in horticulture to propagate true-to-type plants (e.g., mango, citrus)
  • Nucellar seedlings are virus-free
  • Tool in plant breeding for obtaining homozygous lines

Seed Germination — Epigeal & Hypogeal

🌱 "Germination is the seed's act of pure courage — choosing to reach for the light!" 🌱

📋 Conditions for Germination

  • Water (Imbibition) — activates enzymes, swells seed
  • Oxygen — for aerobic respiration (energy)
  • Suitable temperature — optimum varies by species
  • Viable seed — embryo must be alive
  • Some seeds need light (Lactuca) or cold stratification (Rosa)
  • Dormancy must be broken (mechanical/chemical)

☀️ Epigeal Germination

Cotyledons emerge above ground due to rapid elongation of the hypocotyl.

  • Hypocotyl elongates and bends into a hook (plumular hook)
  • Cotyledons pulled above soil → turn green → photosynthesise
  • Cotyledons become first photosynthetic organs

Examples: Bean (Phaseolus), castor, onion, cotton, gourd

🌙 Hypogeal Germination

Cotyledons remain below ground; epicotyl elongates, carrying the plumule above soil.

  • Epicotyl forms an arch and pulls plumule upward
  • Cotyledons stay underground as food reserves
  • First green structure = foliage leaves (not cotyledons)

Examples: Pea (Pisum), maize, wheat, gram, mango

📊 Epigeal vs Hypogeal

FeatureEpigealHypogeal
Part elongatingHypocotylEpicotyl
CotyledonsAbove groundBelow ground
1st green organCotyledonsFoliage leaves
Hook formed byHypocotylEpicotyl
ExampleBean, castorPea, maize
🌸 Vivipary: A special type of germination where seeds germinate while still attached to the parent plant (e.g., mangroves like Rhizophora). The seedling (propagule) drops into the mud and anchors itself — an amazing adaptation to tidal habitats!

Apomixis

✨ "Apomixis is nature's way of cloning perfection — no fertilization needed!" ✨

📖 Definition

Apomixis is the production of seeds without fertilization. The embryo forms without syngamy — a form of asexual reproduction in seeds.

  • Term coined by Winkler (1908)
  • Produces offspring genetically identical to the mother
  • Common in: Citrus, Mangifera, Taraxacum, Poa, grasses

🌸 Types of Apomixis

  • Agamospermy — seeds formed without fertilization
    • Diplospory — unreduced embryo sac from MMC
    • Apospory — embryo sac from somatic (nucellar) cells
    • Adventive embryony — embryo from nucellus/integument cells
  • Vegetative apomixis — vegetative propagules (bulbils) instead of seeds
  • Parthenogenesis — development of egg without fertilization

🌾 Agricultural Importance

  • Hybrid varieties lose vigour on selfing (F₂ breakdown)
  • Apomictic hybrids → fix hybrid vigour permanently
  • Farmers can save and replant seeds without losing trait quality
  • Reduces the cost of hybrid seed production
  • Active research area: introducing apomixis into crop plants (rice, wheat)

🔬 Apomixis vs Normal Seed

FeatureNormalApomictic
FertilizationRequiredAbsent
Genetic variationPresentAbsent (clonal)
Ploidy of embryo2n (from zygote)2n (from somatic cell)
Hybrid vigourLost in F₂Fixed permanently
ExampleMost plantsCitrus, dandelion
💡 Key distinction: Parthenogenesis = egg develops without fertilization (haploid, unless unreduced egg). Adventive embryony = embryo from nucellus/integument (diploid, genetically identical to mother). The latter is most common and agriculturally useful.
