🌿 Types of Classification 🌿

🎭 Artificial Classification

Based on one or very few morphological characters, chosen for convenience rather than natural relationships. Often groups unrelated plants together.

  • Proposed by early botanists like Theophrastus (habit — tree/shrub/herb) and Linnaeus (sexual system — number of stamens)
  • Linnaeus grouped plants into 24 classes based solely on stamen number (e.g., Monandria = 1 stamen, Diandria = 2 stamens…)
  • Simple & practical for identification, but unnatural — e.g., rose and wheat were placed together!
📌
Easy to use
⚠️
Unnatural groups

🌱 Natural Classification

Based on overall morphological similarity — considers many characters (vegetative + floral + fruit) together. Groups plants that share the most features.

  • Proposed by de Jussieu, de Candolle, Bentham & Hooker
  • Reflects natural relationships better than artificial systems
  • Does NOT consider evolutionary history — groups are based on shared appearance, not ancestry
  • Still very useful practically — most classic flora books use this
🌿
Many characters
📖
Classic floras

🧬 Phylogenetic Classification

Based on evolutionary relationships — groups organisms by common ancestry and shared derived characters (synapomorphies). Most scientifically accurate.

  • Proposed by Engler & Prantl, Cronquist, Takhtajan; modern version = APG system
  • Uses molecular data (DNA sequences), shared derived characters, fossil records
  • Produces cladograms showing branching evolutionary history
  • APG IV (2016) is the current standard — completely reshuffled many families!

📚 Bentham & Hooker System 📚

🌿 Introduction

George Bentham and Joseph Dalton Hooker published their classification in the monumental work "Genera Plantarum" (3 volumes, 1862–1883). It is a natural system based on careful study of actual specimens, covering 202 orders and 7,569 genera.

It divides the flowering plants (Angiosperms) into Dicotyledonae and Monocotyledonae, then into series, then cohorts, then orders (= families), then genera.

🌼 Buttercup

1. Ranunculaceae 🌼

Buttercup Family · ~2,500 species · Cosmopolitan (mostly temperate)
🌡️
Temperate
🌿
Herbaceous
🔢
Polypetalae
⊕ K5 C5 A∞ G∞ (apocarpous)

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Mostly herbaceous, rarely shrubby; some aquatic (Ranunculus aquatilis)
  • Leaves alternate, simple or compound, exstipulate, with sheathing base
  • Flowers actinomorphic (zygomorphic in Delphinium, Aconitum), bisexual, hypogynous
  • Perianth of 5 free sepals (petaloid in some) + 5 free petals (sometimes absent)
  • Stamens numerous, spirally arranged, free (polyandrous)
  • Carpels numerous, free (apocarpous gynoecium) — key diagnostic feature!
  • Fruit: achene or follicle (rarely berry in Actaea)
  • Nectaries often present at petal base

💰 Economic Importance

  • Ornamentals: Delphinium, Anemone, Clematis, Aquilegia, Ranunculus — widely grown in gardens 🌸
  • Medicinal: Aconitum napellus (Monkshood) — source of aconitine (analgesic, cardiac drug in controlled doses)
  • Hydrastis canadensis — goldenseal, antifungal/antibacterial
  • Toxic plants: Many are highly poisonous — Aconitum, Helleborus, Ranunculus contain toxic alkaloids
  • Nigella sativa — black seed / kalonji, used as spice and in Ayurveda 🌿
🌼 Ranunculus (Buttercup) 💜 Delphinium 🌸 Anemone 🌿 Clematis ⚗️ Aconitum 🖤 Nigella sativa
🥦 Mustard

2. Brassicaceae 🥦

Mustard / Crucifer Family · ~3,700 species · Mostly North Temperate
✚
Cross-shaped
6️⃣
Tetradynamous
🥗
Veggies!
⊕ K2+2 C4 A2+4 G(2) — Siliqua/Silicula

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Mostly herbs, rarely shrubs; often with pungent smell (glucosinolates/mustard oil)
  • Leaves alternate, simple, exstipulate; often hairy (stellate hairs)
  • Inflorescence: raceme (ebracteate, elongates as flowers open)
  • Flowers actinomorphic, bisexual, cruciform — 4 petals arranged in a cross ✚
  • Sepals 4 (2+2), Petals 4 (free, with claw)
  • Stamens 6, tetradynamous — 4 long inner + 2 short outer 🎯 KEY feature!
  • Gynoecium: bicarpellary, syncarpous, with a false septum (replum)
  • Fruit: Siliqua (long, >3× wide) or Silicula (short, ≤3× wide)

💰 Economic Importance

  • Vegetables: Cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, kale (Brassica oleracea); Radish (Raphanus sativus); Turnip (Brassica rapa) 🥦
  • Oils: Mustard oil (Brassica nigra, B. juncea) — major edible oil in India; Canola/Rapeseed oil (B. napus)
  • Condiments: Mustard seeds as spice 🌶️
  • Ornamentals: Alyssum, Iberis, Matthiola, Lunaria
  • Model plant: Arabidopsis thaliana — the "lab rat" of plant biology 🧬
🥦 Brassica oleracea 🌿 B. juncea (mustard) 🥕 Raphanus sativus 🌸 Alyssum 🧬 Arabidopsis
🌺 Hibiscus

3. Malvaceae 🌺

Mallow / Cotton Family · ~4,300 species · Tropical & Subtropical
🧵
Cotton!
🌀
Monadelphous
⭐
Epicalyx
⊕ Epicalyx K(5) C5 A(∞) G(5) — Loculicidal capsule

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Herbs, shrubs or trees; stem often with mucilaginous sap
  • Leaves alternate, simple, palmately lobed, stipulate; stellate hairs present 🌟
  • Epicalyx (extra calyx-like whorl of bracts) present — KEY feature!
  • Flowers large, showy, actinomorphic, bisexual, pentamerous
  • Petals 5, contorted aestivation (twisted in bud)
  • Stamens ∞, filaments fused into a staminal tube (monadelphous) — wraps around style 🎯
  • Gynoecium: 5–∞ carpels, syncarpous, superior ovary, axile placentation
  • Fruit: loculicidal capsule (cotton) or schizocarp

💰 Economic Importance

  • Fibre: Gossypium hirsutum (cotton) — most important plant fibre in the world! 🧵
  • Vegetable: Abelmoschus esculentus (Okra/Bhindi/Lady's finger) 🥒
  • Fibre & food: Hibiscus cannabinus (Kenaf) — bast fibre
  • Ornamentals: Hibiscus rosa-sinensis (Shoe flower), Althaea, Lavatera 🌺
  • Medicinal: Althaea officinalis (Marshmallow) — mucilaginous root used for sore throat
  • Timber: Tilia (Linden wood)
🧵 Gossypium (Cotton) 🥒 Abelmoschus (Bhindi) 🌺 Hibiscus 🌸 Althaea 🌿 Abutilon

4. Leguminosae / Fabaceae 🫛

Pea / Bean / Pulse Family · ~20,000 species · Worldwide (3rd largest family!)
🦋
Papilionaceous
🧬
N₂ Fixation
9️⃣+1️⃣
Diadelphous
% K(5) C1+2+(2) A(9)+1 G1 — Legume fruit
Subfamily Papilionoideae shown above

🔬 Diagnostic Features (Papilionoideae)

  • Herbs, shrubs, trees, or climbers; root nodules with Rhizobium 🦠
  • Leaves alternate, compound (pinnate or trifoliate), stipulate; pulvinus present
  • Inflorescence: raceme or panicle
  • Flowers zygomorphic, bisexual — papilionaceous corolla:
    1 Standard (vexillum) + 2 Wings (alae) + 2 Keel (carina) petals 🦋
  • Stamens 10, diadelphous (9+1) — 9 fused + 1 free 🎯 KEY!
  • Gynoecium: monocarpellary, superior, marginal placentation
  • Fruit: Legume/Pod — dehisces along both sutures

💰 Economic Importance

  • Food (pulses/protein): Pea (Pisum), Chickpea (Cicer), Soybean (Glycine), Groundnut (Arachis), Lentil (Lens), Beans (Phaseolus) 🫛
  • Nitrogen fixation: Root nodules enrich soil — natural fertiliser! 🌱
  • Timber: Dalbergia (Rosewood, Sheesham), Tectona — premium wood
  • Dyes: Indigofera tinctoria (Indigo dye)
  • Gums: Acacia — gum arabic; Cyamopsis — guar gum
  • Ornamentals: Lathyrus (Sweet pea), Delonix (Gulmohar) 🌸
🫛 Pisum sativum 🥜 Arachis hypogaea 🌿 Cicer arietinum 🌸 Delonix regia 🪵 Dalbergia 💙 Indigofera
🌿 Mint / Tulsi

5. Lamiaceae 🌿

Mint / Sage / Deadnettle Family · ~7,000 species · Worldwide
⬛
Square Stem
👄
Bilabiate
🌺
Aromatic
% K(5) C(2+3) A4 G(2) — 4 nutlets (schizocarp)

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Herbs or shrubs; stems distinctly quadrangular (4-angled/square) 🎯 KEY!
  • Leaves opposite or whorled, simple, exstipulate; aromatic (essential oil glands)
  • Inflorescence: verticillaster (false whorls = 2 cymes in axils)
  • Flowers zygomorphic, bisexual
  • Corolla bilabiate (2-lipped) — 2 upper + 3 lower petals fused 👄
  • Stamens 4, didynamous (2 long + 2 short), epipetalous
  • Gynoecium: bicarpellary, syncarpous, deeply 4-lobed, style gynobasic
  • Fruit: 4 nutlets (schizocarp) — each containing 1 seed

💰 Economic Importance

  • Culinary herbs: Ocimum basilicum (Basil/Tulsi), Mentha (Mint), Rosmarinus (Rosemary), Thymus (Thyme), Salvia (Sage) 🌿
  • Essential oils: Peppermint oil (Mentha piperita), Lavender oil (Lavandula), Thymol from thyme
  • Medicinal: Tulsi (Ocimum sanctum) — immunomodulator, antistress; Salvia officinalis — antiseptic
  • Ornamentals: Lavandula, Salvia, Nepeta (Catnip) 💜
  • Religious: Tulsi is sacred in Hinduism 🙏
🌿 Ocimum (Tulsi) 🍃 Mentha (Mint) 💜 Lavandula 🌸 Salvia 🌿 Thymus 🌺 Rosmarinus
🍅 Tomato / Potato

6. Solanaceae 🍅

Nightshade / Potato Family · ~2,700 species · Mainly Tropical America
🥔
Potato!
🫐
Berry fruit
⚡
Epipetalous
⊕ K(5) C(5) A5 G(2) — Berry / Capsule

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Herbs, shrubs, or small trees; often hairy or viscid
  • Leaves alternate, simple, exstipulate (sometimes pinnately compound in potato)
  • Flowers actinomorphic, bisexual, pentamerous
  • Calyx persistent in fruit (useful character!)
  • Corolla sympetalous (fused petals), rotate/tubular, 5-lobed
  • Stamens 5, epipetalous, alternating with corolla lobes; anthers connivent (cone-like)
  • Gynoecium: bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior, bilocular; axile placentation
  • Fruit: berry (tomato, brinjal) or capsule (Datura, Petunia)

💰 Economic Importance

  • Food crops: Potato (Solanum tuberosum), Tomato (Lycopersicon esculentum), Brinjal (S. melongena), Chilli & Capsicum (Capsicum annuum) 🍅🥔
  • Tobacco: Nicotiana tabacum — cigarettes, nicotine (also used as insecticide) 🚬
  • Medicinal alkaloids: Atropine & hyoscine from Atropa belladonna and Datura — used in ophthalmology and anaesthesia
  • Ornamentals: Petunia, Cestrum, Schizanthus 🌸
  • Spice: Paprika from Capsicum

🌻 Sunflower

7. Asteraceae 🌻

Sunflower / Daisy / Composite Family · ~23,000 species · Worldwide (LARGEST dicot family!)
🌻
Largest!
🎯
Capitulum
🌂
Pappus
Disc: ⊕ K pappus C(5) A(5) G(2̄) · Ray: ↑ K pappus C(3) A0 G(2̄)

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Herbs, rarely shrubs; often with latex or resin canals
  • Leaves mostly alternate, simple, exstipulate
  • Inflorescence: Capitulum (head) — many small flowers (florets) on a flat/convex receptacle surrounded by involucre of bracts 🎯 KEY!
  • Two types of florets:
    • Ray florets (ligulate): strap-shaped, pistillate/neutral, at periphery 🌼
    • Disc florets (tubular): bisexual, at centre 🟤
  • Stamens 5, syngenesious (anthers fused into tube, filaments free)
  • Gynoecium: bicarpellary, inferior ovary, 1 ovule
  • Fruit: cypsela with persistent pappus (feathery, aids dispersal by wind 🌬️)

💰 Economic Importance

  • Oils: Sunflower oil (Helianthus annuus), Safflower oil (Carthamus) 🌻
  • Food: Lettuce (Lactuca sativa), Globe artichoke (Cynara), Chicory (Cichorium — coffee substitute)
  • Ornamentals: Marigold (Tagetes), Chrysanthemum, Dahlia, Zinnia, Gerbera 🌸
  • Medicinal: Artemisia (Artemisinin — malaria drug!), Echinacea (immune booster), Chamomile (Matricaria)
  • Insecticides: Pyrethrin from Tanacetum/Chrysanthemum
🌻 Helianthus 🌼 Tagetes (Marigold) 💐 Dahlia 🥗 Lactuca ⚕️ Artemisia 🌸 Chrysanthemum
🌷 Lily

8. Liliaceae 🌷

Lily Family · ~4,000 species · Mostly Temperate & Subtropical (Monocot)
6️⃣
6 Tepals
🧅
Bulb/Corm
🌿
Monocot
⊕ P(3+3) A(3+3) G(3̄) — Berry or Capsule

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Perennial herbs with underground bulb, corm, or rhizome
  • Leaves simple, often linear with parallel venation (monocot!); alternate or basal
  • Flowers large, showy, actinomorphic, bisexual
  • Perianth of 6 tepals in 2 whorls of 3 (petals & sepals alike = petaloid tepals) 🎯
  • Stamens 6 (3+3), free or epitepalous
  • Gynoecium: tricarpellary, syncarpous, superior ovary (inferior in some), axile placentation
  • Fruit: berry (Asparagus, Smilax) or loculicidal capsule (Lilium, Allium)

💰 Economic Importance

  • Vegetables: Onion (Allium cepa), Garlic (A. sativum), Asparagus (Asparagus officinalis) 🧅
  • Medicinal: Aloe vera — gel for skin burns, laxative; Colchicum autumnale — colchicine (anti-gout, used in polyploidy breeding!)
  • Ornamentals: Lilium (Lily), Tulipa (Tulip), Hyacinthus (Hyacinth), Gloriosa, Hemerocallis 🌷
  • Spice: Garlic as flavouring & antimicrobial
  • Timber: Yucca — used in construction in dry areas
🧅 Allium cepa 🧄 A. sativum 🌷 Tulipa 🌸 Lilium 💊 Aloe vera 🌿 Asparagus
🌾 Grass Spikelet

9. Poaceae / Gramineae 🌾

Grass Family · ~11,000 species · Worldwide (Economically MOST important family!)
🌾
Rice & Wheat
🔲
Hollow stem
🌀
Spikelet
Floret: P lodicules(2–3) A3 G(2̄) — Caryopsis (grain)

🔬 Diagnostic Features

  • Herbs (rarely woody — bamboo); stems = culm: hollow internodes, solid nodes 🎯
  • Leaves with sheathing base, open leaf sheath, ligule at junction of sheath & blade
  • Inflorescence: spikelet — basic unit bearing florets between lemma & palea (modified bracts)
  • Flowers small, wind-pollinated; perianth reduced to 2–3 lodicules
  • Stamens usually 3, versatile anthers (dangling)
  • Gynoecium: bicarpellary, syncarpous, superior; feathery (plumose) stigma — catches airborne pollen 🌬️
  • Fruit: Caryopsis (grain) — pericarp fused with seed coat 🌾

💰 Economic Importance

  • Cereals (staple food): Rice (Oryza sativa), Wheat (Triticum), Maize (Zea mays), Sorghum, Barley, Oats, Millet — feed most of humanity! 🌾
  • Sugar: Sugarcane (Saccharum officinarum) — world's main sugar source 🍬
  • Bamboo: Bambusa — construction, furniture, paper, food (bamboo shoots)
  • Fodder: Cynodon (Bermuda grass), Pennisetum — livestock feed
  • Biofuel: Switchgrass (Panicum virgatum), Sugarcane bagasse
  • Lawn & Sports turf: Cynodon, Festuca, Poa
🌾 Oryza sativa (Rice) 🍞 Triticum (Wheat) 🌽 Zea mays (Maize) 🍬 Saccharum 🎋 Bambusa 🍺 Hordeum (Barley)
